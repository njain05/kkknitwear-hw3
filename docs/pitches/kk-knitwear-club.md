# Pitch: K.K Knitwear Club

**Product being pitched:** knitted fabric, sold by the kilo
**Pitched to:** a garment manufacturer or sportswear brand sourcing cloth
**Not:** a shopper. Nobody buys 500 kg of dot knit for themselves.

> Practice note: the USP below is not invented. It is drawn from what this
> business actually is — a ~Rs 1.5–5 Cr proprietorship with 11–25 staff, running
> its own knitting machines in Ludhiana since 1990. Everything claimed here is
> something they could deliver on Monday.

---

## The USP in one line

**"We'll run the order a larger mill won't take."**

Tagline used on the site: *Mill-direct knitted fabric. Small lots, quick turnaround.*

---

## Why this is the right USP

A buyer choosing a fabric supplier is really choosing between three kinds:

| Supplier type | Their strength | Where they fail the buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Large mill | Price at volume, certifications | Won't take 200 kg. Long queue for a knitting slot. |
| Trader / agent | Wide range, no minimums | Doesn't own machines. Margin on top. Can't control quality or timing. |
| **K.K Knitwear Club** | **Owns the machines AND takes the small order** | Can't compete on a 50-tonne contract — and shouldn't try |

The gap K.K sits in is real and defensible: **a manufacturer that behaves like a
trader on order size, and like a mill on control and price.**

This is achievable *today*, with no new investment. It is simply what a
25-person works with its own machines can do that a 500-person mill cannot be
bothered to.

### What NOT to claim
- No specific certifications (Oeko-Tex, GOTS, ISO) — they don't have them, and
  a garment exporter will ask for the certificate within two messages.
- No "premium" or "luxury" positioning — it invites exactly those questions.
- No export capability claims.
- No specific delivery time until a lot size is known.

The register is: **established, capable, easy to deal with.** Not cheapest,
not finest.

## Objection handling

**"Your rate is higher than the mill quoted me."**
> On a full container it would be. On 300 kilos the mill won't quote you at all,
> and by the time a trader has added his margin you're above us anyway. Compare
> us to what you'd actually pay for this quantity.

**"Can you handle my volume if we grow?"**
> Tell me the number and I'll tell you honestly whether it's a fit. I'd rather
> say no to one order than miss a delivery and lose you.

**"Do you have Oeko-Tex / GOTS?"**
> Not currently. If your buyer requires certified fabric I'm not the right
> supplier for that programme — but for domestic and non-certified lines we're
> straightforward to work with.
> *(Honesty here wins the next order. Bluffing loses the account.)*

**"How do I know the quality?"**
> Take a swatch before you commit. Every quality on our list can be sampled.

**"I already have a supplier."**
> Keep him. Use us for the runs he won't take — the short ones and the urgent
> ones. That's where we're worth having in your contacts.

## Who to target first

1. **Small and mid garment units in Ludhiana and Tiruppur** — the exact people
   whose order size gets refused elsewhere
2. **Sportswear and team-kit makers** — repeat orders, colour-driven, urgent
3. **School uniform suppliers** — seasonal, house colours, predictable repeats
4. **Home furnishing makers** — pillow and cover cloth, steady volume

Least promising: large exporters. They will ask for certifications first.

## How the website carries this pitch

The site was built to make exactly this argument without a phone call:

- Headline is the USP, not the company name
- "Small lots welcome" is the first of three reasons given on the homepage
- Filtering by GSM, width and structure mirrors how a buyer actually specifies
- The quote basket lets a buyer ask about several qualities in one message
- Call / WhatsApp / Quote stay on screen on mobile throughout
- No prices published — every quality is quoted per lot, which is how this
  trade actually works and keeps negotiating room intact
